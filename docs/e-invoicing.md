# E-invoicing platform

Decision record for issue #519, written on 2026-10-16. It picks the
*plateforme agréée* Opusline connects to first, and defines the contract every
platform adapter implements (`app/Domain/EInvoicing/Platform/EInvoicingPlatform.php`).

## Why a platform at all

Since 1 September 2026 every VAT-registered business in France, micro-entrepreneurs
included, must be able to **receive** electronic invoices. From 1 September 2027
they must also **send** theirs that way, and report e-reporting data. Invoices do
not travel between businesses directly: each side uses a *plateforme agréée*
(PA, formerly PDP), registered with the DGFiP, and the platforms talk to each
other and to the public *annuaire*. The public portal stopped carrying invoices in
October 2024; it only keeps the annuaire.

Becoming a platform takes a state registration, an audit and a company behind
it, so Opusline cannot be one. It connects to one, through its API, the way it
reads bank movements through Enable Banking: with the **user's own credentials**,
so a self-hosted instance never depends on an account Opusline would hold.

## What Opusline needs from a platform

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| Public, documented API with a sandbox | Opusline is code, not a person clicking in a web app; the adapter has to be testable without a real company. |
| Credentials a user can create alone | Each account connects its own platform account from « Réglages → Intégrations », as with Enable Banking and the Anthropic key. No partnership contract, no shared secret in the image. |
| Receiving + the mandatory statuses | #521: supplier invoices land in Dépenses; the account must be able to approve, refuse and mark them paid. |
| Sending + lifecycle reading | #523 and #524: issued invoices leave through the platform; « encaissée » is reported from Opusline, where the payment is recorded. |
| Annuaire lookup | Knowing before sending whether a client can receive electronic invoices at all. |
| E-reporting | #525: B2C, EU and export operations that carry no invoice. |
| A free tier for one micro-business | Opusline's users send tens of invoices a year, not thousands. |
| Terms that allow a third-party, self-hosted, AGPL application | The adapter runs on the user's server, under their account; nothing in the terms may require the software vendor to hold the credentials. |

## Decision

**Super PDP is the first adapter.** It is the only registered platform that is
free at Opusline's scale, API-first with a published OpenAPI document and a
sandbox, and whose authentication model lets each user mint their own
credentials. The user creates a Super PDP account, verifies their company,
creates an application with the three scopes, and pastes its client id and
secret into « Réglages → Intégrations »; Opusline stores them encrypted like the
Enable Banking key and runs every call as that company.

**B2Brouter is the second adapter**, for users who prefer a platform with
webhooks and a longer track record, and as the proof that the contract is not
Super PDP's shape. It is not built in #520; the contract is written so that it
could be.

**The contract is the reform's, not a vendor's.** `EInvoicingPlatform` names
what every platform must offer: connection check, annuaire lookup, send,
status read, received invoices list and download, status report. Statuses are
the CDV codes (`LifecycleStatus`). Nothing in the interface mentions OAuth,
webhooks or JSON.

## What #520 has to confirm before building

- **Terms.** Super PDP published its *conditions générales* on 11 December
  2025. Read them for anything that restricts third-party software or requires
  the software vendor to be the contracting party; the OAuth documentation and
  the vendor's own answer to an open-source integrator suggest the opposite,
  but the record above rests on that, not on the text.
- **Webhooks.** Super PDP's API had none as of the Ruby client's last check;
  reception is polled. #521 therefore runs like the bank sync: a nightly
  scheduler job plus a manual « Sync » button.
- **E-reporting.** B2C support arrived in July 2026 and mixed invoices (goods
  and services) are reported as unsupported by one comparator; #525 checks the
  current changelog.
- **The sandbox.** It shares the production host and is selected by the
  credentials. The adapter takes a base URL and a credentials pair, nothing
  else, so tests and CI can point at either.
- **Pricing.** The free online account covers Opusline's volumes; the per-invoice
  API rate applies beyond it. State it on the Integrations tab so nobody is
  surprised.

## Setting it up

Each Opusline account connects its own Super PDP account; on an instance shared
by several people each one sets up theirs. Nothing in the setup costs anything
at a freelancer's volumes.

1. **Create a Super PDP account** at [app.superpdp.com](https://app.superpdp.com)
   with your SIRET and VAT number.
2. **Let Super PDP verify your company.** Under its settings, upload the legal
   representative's identity document and a recent Kbis. It takes one to three
   days; until it is done, Super PDP refuses every business call, and Opusline
   shows the connection as « awaiting verification ».
3. **Register in the annuaire** from Super PDP's Directory page. That is what
   lets other platforms route invoices to you; Opusline shows the resulting
   receiving address on the Integrations tab.
4. **Create an application** under Developers → Applications, with the scopes
   `invoice:read`, `invoice:write` and `company:read`. Super PDP shows the
   client secret once.
5. **Paste the client ID and secret** under **Settings → Integrations →
   E-invoicing** and connect. Opusline asks Super PDP about the application
   before saving, stores the secret encrypted, and shows the company, whether
   it is verified, its environment (sandbox or production: the credentials
   decide) and its receiving address. **Test the connection** asks again at any
   time, for instance once the verification completed.

Sandbox credentials work the same way and file nothing with the administration:
useful to try the flow before the company is verified in production.

## Receiving supplier invoices

Once the platform verified the company, Opusline reads the inbox every night at
05:30 (server time), after the bank sync, and **Read now** on the Dépenses page
does the same on demand. Every supplier invoice the platform holds becomes one
expense:

- the supplier, the invoice number, the date, the amounts and the VAT rate come
  from the invoice's structured data, so nothing is read off the PDF;
- the invoice's own PDF is filed as the receipt;
- the category starts as « Autre » for you to set;
- an invoice dated in the future is dated today;
- an invoice in another currency than the account's is left aside.

A received invoice is recorded once, whatever later syncs see; a later sync only
follows its status (acknowledged, approved, refused, paid…), shown on the row.
Deleting such an expense keeps it from coming back. When the platform refuses a
run, the Dépenses page says why, and Réglages → Intégrations carries the same
error.

## Where the pieces go

- `app/Domain/EInvoicing/Platform/` — the contract and its value objects
  (`RecipientLookup`, `SubmittedInvoice`, `ReceivedInvoice`,
  `EInvoicingPlatformException`).
- `app/Domain/EInvoicing/Enums/LifecycleStatus.php` — CDV codes.
- `app/Domain/EInvoicing/SuperPdp/` — the Super PDP client and adapter; the
  per-user credentials live in `e_invoicing_connections`, one row per account.
- `app/Domain/Expenses/` — the receiving side (#521) consumes `ReceivedInvoice`
  and the Factur-X file through the existing receipt pipeline.
